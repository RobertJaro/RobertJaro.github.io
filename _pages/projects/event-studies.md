**Follow the magnetic field before, during, and after a solar eruption.** These studies connect three-dimensional magnetic reconstructions with the flare ribbons, hot plasma, and filament motions recorded by solar telescopes.

## The magnetic engine of the May 2024 eruptions

Our study of **NOAA active region 13664**, the source of the May 2024 extreme geomagnetic storm, reconstructs its magnetic evolution from May 5 to May 11 at the **12-minute cadence** of SDO/HMI.

Every analyzed X-class flare was associated with a decrease in the ratio of free to total magnetic energy. Maps of energy depletion also corresponded to observed EUV brightenings, linking the reconstructed energy release to specific locations in the active region. The detailed May 10 case reveals interacting magnetic domains and substantial changes in their connectivity.

The study also introduced a vector-potential formulation that makes the reconstructed magnetic field divergence-free. These extrapolations provide a sequence of constrained magnetic states; interpreting the fast eruption itself requires comparison with observations and dynamical models.

## Extending the method across magnetic environments

**A 500 Mm filament in weak magnetic fields.** Our collaborative 2025 study reconstructed a giant flux rope extending far beyond an active-region core. Its different connections at either end help explain why the eastern leg erupted freely and produced extensive coronal dimming while the western side remained partly confined.

**Reconnection inside a compact magnetic dome.** Combining DKIST observations, spectral inversions, and NF2 revealed a fan-spine magnetic configuration with a null point about **500 km above the input magnetogram**. Local heating and rapid plasma flows support an interpretation of magnetic reconnection in the lower atmosphere.
