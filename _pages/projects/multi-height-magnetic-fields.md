**A second view of the magnetic field can change the entire three-dimensional picture.** By combining measurements from the photosphere and chromosphere, we constrain the magnetic structures that store energy before an eruption.

## Revealing a filament's magnetic flux rope · 2026

Does a solar filament sit inside an existing twisted flux rope, or does the rope form during eruption? Our **2026 Astrophysical Journal Letters study** shows why chromospheric observations matter for answering this question. In radiative-MHD benchmarks, surface-only extrapolations can misidentify the configuration, while adding chromospheric vector measurements recovers the structure more accurately.

Applied to Swedish Solar Telescope observations of active region 13392, the method reconstructs **dipped, twisted field lines consistent with a pre-eruptive flux rope**. It jointly accounts for uncertain measurement heights and the 180-degree ambiguity in the transverse magnetic field.

## Making better use of measurements at different heights · 2024

The foundation is a flexible height-mapping model that adjusts where each magnetic measurement belongs in the atmosphere. This matters because spectral lines sample corrugated layers rather than flat surfaces.

Our **2024 study** demonstrated improved magnetic reconstructions in analytical and MURaM benchmarks. Combining SDO/HMI photospheric measurements with SOLIS/VSM chromospheric observations also produced a field configuration in active region 11166 that better matched observed EUV structures.
